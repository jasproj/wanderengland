15 Best Things to Do in York, England

Medieval walls, Viking history, Gothic grandeur — England's best-preserved old city

York is 2,000 years of history stacked in layers you can actually see. Roman walls hold up medieval gates. Viking streets run beneath Georgian townhouses. A Gothic cathedral the size of a football pitch rises over timber-framed pubs. It's one of the most walkable cities in England — you can cover the entire old town in an afternoon, though you'll want longer.

Two hours by train from London, York makes an excellent overnight trip or a base for exploring the Yorkshire Moors and Dales.

1. York Minster

The largest Gothic cathedral in northern Europe

York Minster took 250 years to build and contains more medieval stained glass than any other church in England — including the Great East Window, the size of a tennis court. The Undercroft museum reveals Roman, Saxon, and Norman foundations beneath the floor. Climb the 275 steps to the top of the Central Tower for views across the city and the Vale of York beyond.

~£16 adults · Tower extra · Allow 2 hours
Tip: Evensong (usually 5:15pm) is free and gives you the Minster's acoustics at their best. No booking needed — arrive 15 minutes early.

2. Walk the City Walls

The most complete medieval walls in England

York's city walls run for 2.5 miles around the old town — you can walk the full circuit in about two hours. The best section runs from Bootham Bar (near the Minster) to Monk Bar, with views of the Minster on one side and the Yorkshire countryside on the other. The four medieval "bars" (gates) are worth stopping at, especially Monk Bar with its working portcullis.

Free · 2 hours full circuit

3. The Shambles

Medieval street — and the inspiration for Diagon Alley

The Shambles dates to the 14th century and is one of the best-preserved medieval streets in Europe. The timber-framed buildings lean toward each other across the narrow lane, almost touching at the top. It was originally a street of butchers' shops — the wide window sills were meat shelves. Now it's independent shops, fudge makers, and a Harry Potter–themed store that draws the crowds. Come early morning for photos without people.

Free · Best before 9am

4. JORVIK Viking Centre

Time travel to Viking-age York

Built on the actual archaeological site where Viking-age houses, workshops, and artifacts were excavated in the 1970s, JORVIK takes you through a reconstructed Viking street in a ride car — complete with sounds, smells, and animatronic figures. The museum section displays over 40,000 objects found on the site. It's more serious than it sounds — the archaeology behind it is world-class.

~£14 adults · Allow 1 hour

5. York's Snickelways

Hidden alleys and passages through the old town

York's "snickelways" — a word coined by local writer Mark Jones — are the narrow alleys, ginnels, and passages that thread between the main streets. Some are medieval; some are even older. Coffee Yard, Lady Peckett's Yard, and Mad Alice Lane are among the best. There's no map that catches them all — the joy is in getting slightly lost. A guided walking tour is the best way to find the hidden ones.

Free · Walking tours from ~£10

6. Clifford's Tower

Norman keep on a Viking mound

The last remaining tower of York Castle sits on a steep mound in the center of the city. The views from the top cover the Minster, the city walls, and the surrounding countryside. The tower also marks one of the darkest moments in English history — the 1190 massacre of York's Jewish community. The exhibition inside addresses this history with care and clarity.

~£8 adults

7. National Railway Museum

The world's largest railway museum — and it's free

Even if you're not a train enthusiast, this museum is impressive. The Great Hall holds locomotives from the 1820s to the present, including Mallard (world speed record holder for steam), the only Japanese bullet train outside Japan, and a section of the Channel Tunnel. The Station Hall recreates a Victorian station platform. Allow longer than you think.

Free · Allow 2–3 hours

8. York Castle Museum

Everyday life through the centuries

A recreated Victorian street — Kirkgate — runs through the center of the museum, with period shopfronts you can enter. Beyond that: rooms furnished from different eras, a collection of everyday objects, and a wing covering 1960s York. The museum occupies the former debtors' prison and the old Female Prison, which adds atmosphere. Dick Turpin was held here before his execution.

~£13 adults · Allow 1.5–2 hours

9. Betty's Café Tea Rooms

Yorkshire institution since 1919

Betty's on St Helen's Square is an Art Deco tea room that's been serving Yorkshire Tea, cakes, and the Fat Rascal (a giant fruit scone) since 1919. There's almost always a queue — it moves steadily and the experience is worth the wait. The downstairs Belmont Room is quieter if you don't want to queue. Don't skip the cakes — the pastry chefs are Swiss-trained.

Afternoon tea ~£25 · Expect a queue

10. York's Chocolate Story

The city that gave the world Kit Kat and Smarties

Rowntree's (now Nestlé) and Terry's both started in York — the city has a serious chocolate heritage. This guided tour covers the history from Quaker entrepreneurs to global brands, with tastings and a chocolate-making workshop. It's more engaging than you'd expect, especially the business history of how a few York families changed the confectionery industry.

~£16 adults · 1 hour guided

11. River Ouse Cruise

See York from the water

City Cruises runs 45-minute sightseeing trips along the River Ouse, passing under York's bridges with commentary on the buildings and history along the banks. The evening cruises (with a drink included) are the best value. You can also walk the riverside path from the city center to the Millennium Bridge and back — a flat, scenic loop.

From ~£12 · 45 min

12. The York Dungeon

Theatrical history — plague, Guy Fawkes, and Vikings

Live actors guide you through 2,000 years of York's darkest episodes in a series of themed rooms. It's campy and fun — part haunted house, part history lesson. Guy Fawkes was born in York, and his story gets a full room. Good for older kids and anyone who enjoys interactive theatre. Not a substitute for real history, but entertaining alongside it.

~£15–20 · Allow 1 hour

13. Treasurer's House

National Trust house with a ghost story

This medieval house next to the Minster was restored by Frank Green in the early 1900s with an eclectic collection of furniture and art. The ghost story is the draw — in 1953, a plumber working in the cellar reported seeing a column of Roman soldiers marching through the wall, visible only from the knees up (because the Roman road was below the current floor level). The National Trust tells the story on every tour.

NT members free · ~£9 otherwise

14. York Ghost Walk

England's most haunted city, after dark

York claims to be the most haunted city in England, and the ghost walks lean into it. The Original Ghost Walk of York (running since 1973) is the oldest, starting at the King's Arms pub at 8pm nightly. No booking needed — just show up. The guides are theatrical without overdoing it, and the historical content is solid. The route takes you through the Snickelways and past some of York's spookiest spots.

~£7 · 75 min · Nightly 8pm

15. Day Trip to Castle Howard

One of England's grandest stately homes

Fifteen miles northeast of York, Castle Howard has been the home of the Howard family for over 300 years. You'll recognize it from Brideshead Revisited (both the 1981 series and the 2008 film). The house, gardens, and grounds take a full half-day. The Temple of the Four Winds and the Mausoleum are the architectural highlights. No direct public transport — drive or book a tour from York.

~£22 adults · 30 min drive
