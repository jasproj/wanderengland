15 Best Things to Do in Bath, England

Roman ruins, Georgian crescents, natural hot springs — and why Bath deserves more than a day trip

Bath is the kind of place that looks like a film set and then turns out to be a real, living city. Two thousand years of history packed into honey-colored limestone, from Roman engineering to Georgian townhouses. It's one of the most visited cities in England — and one of the few that fully earns it.

Most visitors come on a day trip from London, and you can absolutely see the highlights in a day. But staying a night or two lets you catch Bath at its best — early morning on the Royal Crescent, a late swim at Thermae Bath Spa, dinner in a restaurant that would hold its own in any major city.

1. The Roman Baths

The original reason Bath exists

Built around natural hot springs that the Romans discovered nearly 2,000 years ago, this is one of the best-preserved Roman sites in northern Europe. The Great Bath, the sacred spring, and the museum of artifacts tell the story of Aquae Sulis — the Roman name for Bath. The audio guide (included) is narrated by Bill Bryson and is genuinely worth listening to.

Allow 90 min–2 hours
Tip: Book a late-afternoon entry — smaller crowds and torchlit atmospherics in summer. Adult tickets around £28; book online to guarantee your time slot.

2. Royal Crescent

The most iconic Georgian architecture in England

Thirty townhouses arranged in a perfect crescent arc, designed by John Wood the Younger and completed in 1774. The lawn in front is public — bring a coffee and sit on the grass. No. 1 Royal Crescent is a museum showing how a wealthy Georgian family lived, with period-accurate rooms and furnishings.

Free to walk · Museum ~£12

3. Thermae Bath Spa

The only place in Britain where you can bathe in natural hot springs

This modern spa uses the same spring water the Romans bathed in, heated naturally to 46°C. The rooftop pool has open-air views over Bath Abbey and the surrounding hills — especially good at dusk. The two-hour Twilight Package is the sweet spot: a swim, steam rooms, and the rooftop pool as the city lights up.

From ~£40 for 2 hours
Tip: Weekday mornings are quietest. Weekend afternoons often sell out — book at least a few days ahead, especially in summer.

4. Bath Abbey

Gothic church at the heart of the city

The Abbey's fan-vaulted ceiling is one of the finest in England. The Tower Tour takes you up 212 steps for views over the city and behind the clock face. The Abbey sits right next to the Roman Baths, so most visitors see both in the same morning. Heritage donation requested at the door.

Tower Tour ~£8

5. Pulteney Bridge and the Weir

One of only four bridges in the world lined with shops on both sides

Designed by Robert Adam in 1774, Pulteney Bridge crosses the River Avon with shops built into both sides — like a smaller Ponte Vecchio. The view from the south side, looking back at the bridge with the horseshoe weir below, is Bath's most photographed spot. Walk across, browse the small shops, then descend to Parade Gardens for the best angle.

Free

6. The Circus

Georgian architecture in the round

Three curved terraces forming a perfect circle, designed by John Wood the Elder. Look up at the carved acorns and oak leaves along the roofline — a nod to the Druids. Three enormous plane trees fill the center. The Circus connects to the Royal Crescent via Brock Street — walk both in one loop.

Free

7. Jane Austen Centre

Bath through Austen's eyes

Austen lived in Bath from 1801 to 1806, and the city features in Northanger Abbey and Persuasion. The Centre covers her life here, the Regency world she wrote about, and the places that inspired her settings. The Regency Tea Room upstairs serves tea with dress-up options. The annual Jane Austen Festival in September fills the streets with bonnets and tailcoats.

~£14 · Allow 1 hour

8. Prior Park Landscape Garden

National Trust garden with a Palladian bridge

A ten-minute walk uphill from the city centre (or take the hop-on bus), this 18th-century garden has one of only four Palladian bridges in the world. The view from the bridge back down over Bath is one of the best in the city. It's a peaceful escape from the crowds — most visitors skip it.

NT members free · ~£10 otherwise

9. Sally Lunn's Historic Eating House

The oldest house in Bath — and its most famous bun

Sally Lunn's has been serving its signature bun from this building since the 1680s. The bun itself is somewhere between bread and brioche — served with sweet or savory toppings. The kitchen museum in the basement shows the original medieval foundations. There's usually a queue, but it moves fast.

Bun from ~£8

10. Bath's Food Scene

Better restaurants than a city this size has any right to

The Circus Restaurant and Menu Gordon Jones offer tasting menus that compete with Bristol and London. For casual eating, the Green Park Brasserie does weekend jazz brunches, and the covered market on Green Street has independent food stalls. Landrace Bakery and Colonna & Smalls are two of the best bakery-café combos in southwest England.

Budget £15–45 per meal

11. Walk the Kennet and Avon Canal

Flat towpath walk out of the city

Pick up the canal path at the bottom of Widcombe and walk east toward Bathampton — about 40 minutes each way, flat the whole distance. Narrowboats, swans, and the George Inn at Bathampton at the turnaround point. It's the best free walk in Bath if you want to get out of the city center without driving anywhere.

Free · 2–3 hours round trip

12. Bath Skyline Walk

National Trust circular route above the city

A six-mile loop through meadows, woodlands, and Iron Age hill forts on the ridgeline above Bath. The views down into the valley are the best you'll get of the city's setting among green hills. Start from Bathwick Hill or the University of Bath campus. Well-signed, moderate difficulty — good footwear recommended after rain.

Free · Allow 2.5–3 hours

13. Victoria Art Gallery

Free gallery next to Pulteney Bridge

Small but well-curated permanent collection including Turner, Gainsborough, and Sickert, plus rotating exhibitions. The gallery sits right on Bridge Street — easy to combine with a walk across Pulteney Bridge. Most visitors walk straight past it, which is their loss.

Free · 30–45 min

14. Day Trip to Castle Combe and Lacock

Two of the prettiest villages in the Cotswolds

Castle Combe — a tiny village in a wooded valley that's been used as a filming location for everything from War Horse to Downton Abbey. Lacock is equally photogenic and home to Lacock Abbey, where Fox Talbot made some of the first photographs. Both are within 30 minutes of Bath by car and make a great half-day loop. No public transport — you'll need to drive or book a tour.

30 min drive from Bath

15. The Holburne Museum

Art museum at the end of Great Pulteney Street

Walk the full length of Great Pulteney Street — one of the widest and grandest streets in Bath — to reach the Holburne Museum. The collection spans silver, ceramics, and paintings from the 17th to 20th centuries. The modern glass extension at the back has a café overlooking Sydney Gardens, Bath's oldest park.

Free · Allow 1 hour
