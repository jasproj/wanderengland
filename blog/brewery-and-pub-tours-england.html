Brewery & Pub Tours in England: London, Liverpool, the Peak District & Beyond (2026)

England has roughly 1,800 breweries and 45,000 pubs, and the guided tours fall into two kinds: a walk between historic pubs with a guide who knows the buildings, or a tasting tour that gets you inside working breweries and distilleries. London has the most of both; Liverpool runs them on a bus; the Peak District does it between villages. Every price below is the operator's own on FareHarbor, checked October 2026. For the pub walks alone, there is a dedicated hub at pub tours.

London

Historic pub walks

The classic London pub walk takes in four or five pubs across Holborn, Fleet Street and Covent Garden: Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese (rebuilt 1667), the Seven Stars (1602), the Lamb & Flag and the Cittie of Yorke. Two and a half hours, a half-pint in each, and the history of the building is the point as much as the beer. Ale Hunters run the small-group version at £46; Experience Local's Great British Pub Tour is the budget option at £31.

Craft brewery tastings

The "Bermondsey Beer Mile" under the railway arches south of Tower Bridge is the best-known crawl, but the guided tours now also cover the Blackhorse Lane cluster in Walthamstow, which has six breweries within a 10-minute walk of each other and no tourists. Expect three breweries, a flight at each and a taproom finish.

The Peak District (from Manchester and Sheffield)

Pub Tours Ltd runs a minibus day out from Bakewell through the Peak District villages: Thornbridge in Bakewell, the Old Hall at Hope, and stone pubs in Eyam and Castleton that have been pouring since the 1600s. It is the nearest thing to a brewery tour from Manchester (an hour away) and the only way to do a Peak pub crawl without a designated driver.

Distilleries: gin in Bristol, Dorset and the New Forest

If you would rather taste spirits than beer, England's small distilleries run short tours with a tasting at the end for around £30. Bristol Dry Gin is a micro-distillery in the city centre; Conker is Dorset's first gin distillery, in Bournemouth; and Jaunt E-Bikes combines a New Forest ride with a beer tasting at the end.

Before you book

  • Eat first. Most pub walks include a half-pint per stop and no food.
  • Age limits are 18+ on every tour here; some operators check ID at the first pub.
  • Private vs public: London pub walks run public most evenings at £31–£46 a head; a private version for a stag or work group is £185–£200 for the party.
  • Sundays: some brewery taprooms close; the walking pub tours still run.

FAQ: Brewery & Pub Tours in England

How much does a brewery tour cost in England?

Guided craft brewery tastings in London are £45 to £55 a person for three breweries with a flight at each. Historic pub walks are £31 to £46. Liverpool's brewery bus is about £100 for four hours including tastings, and distillery tours with a tasting are around £30.

What is the difference between a pub tour and a brewery tour?

A pub tour is a guided walk between historic pubs, usually four or five, with a drink in each and the history of the buildings as the focus. A brewery tour goes behind the scenes at working breweries with tastings at the source. London has both; Liverpool's version is a bus between breweries.

Are there brewery tours in Manchester or York?

Not yet on FareHarbor. The closest bookable option to Manchester is the Peak District pub tour from Bakewell, an hour away. For York, the pub walks on this site are London-based; check back as operators are added.

Can I book a private pub crawl for a stag or hen group?

Yes. London Guided Walks and Brit Icon Tours both run private historic pub walks priced per group (about £185 to £200), and Brewery Bus Tours in Liverpool runs a private four-hour bus for up to 16.
