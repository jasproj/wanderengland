16 Best Things to Do in Cornwall

Cornwall is a 300-mile coastline wrapped around a granite spine, with Atlantic surf on the north coast and sheltered creeks on the south. It is a five-hour drive from London, which is why most people give it a week and base themselves in two places. This list runs roughly east to west and finishes at Land's End; the things worth booking ahead are flagged, because July and August sell out. Tours you can book here →

North Coast and Atlantic

1. Tintagel Castle

Castle English Heritage

The ruins of a 13th-century castle on a headland cut off from the mainland, reached since 2019 by a 70-metre steel footbridge that replaced 148 steps. Excavations found a 5th-century settlement trading with the Mediterranean, which is the real story under the King Arthur one. Merlin's Cave at the base of the cliff is walkable at low tide. Book a timed slot in summer; the car park is in the village, a 15-minute walk away.

2. Surfing at Newquay

Active Book ahead

Fistral is the UK's best-known surf beach and the site of the national championships; Towan and Great Western in the town are gentler and better for a first lesson. A two-hour group lesson includes wetsuit and board, and the Cornish Wave school at Towan is the one that sells out first in school holidays. Water is warmest in September; the swell is biggest from October to March.

Bookable: Two-hour surfing lesson at Newquay (Cornish Wave · from £42) — live availability on FareHarbor.

3. Padstow and the Camel Trail

Harbour Cycle

A fishing harbour with Rick Stein's four restaurants and a chip shop queue to match. The Camel Trail is an 18-mile flat cycle path on the old railway line to Wadebridge and Bodmin, with bike hire at both ends; the 5-mile estuary stretch from Padstow to Wadebridge is the pretty bit. The Padstow ferry crosses to Rock, where the Camel estuary beaches are. The ghost walk runs after dark through the old town.

Bookable: The Ghost Walk Padstow (The Ghost Walk Padstow · from £14) — live availability on FareHarbor.

4. Bude and the Bude Sea Pool

Beach Swim

The northernmost resort in Cornwall, with a semi-natural tidal sea pool cut into the rocks at Summerleaze beach in the 1930s; it is free, 91 metres long, and refilled by every high tide. Surf lessons here are smaller-scale than Newquay and the beaches are wider. The cliffs south of Bude towards Boscastle are the highest in Cornwall; the walk from Crackington Haven to Boscastle is a serious 7 miles.

Bookable: Surf lesson at Bude (Big Blue Surf School · from £44.1) — live availability on FareHarbor.

5. St Ives and Tate St Ives

Town Art

Four beaches around a granite harbour, and the light that drew Ben Nicholson, Barbara Hepworth and Alfred Wallis in the 1930s. Tate St Ives sits above Porthmeor beach; the Barbara Hepworth Museum and Sculpture Garden is her actual studio, left as it was. Park at Lelant Saltings and take the branch-line train in along the coast; the town's car parks fill by mid-morning in summer. Porthminster is the sheltered swimming beach.

6. Seal spotting and wildlife cruises from Falmouth

Boat Wildlife

Falmouth has the third-deepest natural harbour in the world and the boats to use it. Short cruises go up the Fal to the Pandora Inn and Trelissick; the five-hour offshore trips head for the Manacles and beyond for grey seals, common dolphins, basking sharks in June and July, and occasionally minke whales. The Falmouth History Tour on foot covers the packet-ship era and Pendennis Castle.

Bookable: Five-hour offshore whale and dolphin cruise from Falmouth (AK Wildlife Cruises · from £90.1) — live availability on FareHarbor.

West Cornwall and Land's End

7. St Michael's Mount

Island Tide times

A castle and 12th-century priory on a tidal island off Marazion, reached on foot over a granite causeway for about four hours either side of low tide, and by boat the rest of the time. The St Aubyn family has lived in the castle since 1659 and still does. The sub-tropical terraced gardens are open on fewer days than the castle; check both before going. Marazion beach, facing the Mount, is also where to learn paddleboarding on calm days.

Bookable: Private paddleboard intro lesson at Marazion (Ocean High · from £53) — live availability on FareHarbor.

8. The Minack Theatre

Theatre Cliff

An open-air theatre carved into the granite cliff at Porthcurno between 1932 and 1983, almost entirely by one woman, Rowena Cade, and her gardener. It seats 750 above a turquoise cove and stages plays from May to September; bring a cushion and a coat, performances run in rain. Daytime visits to the theatre and its sub-tropical garden are possible when there is no matinee. Porthcurno beach below is the finest in west Cornwall.

9. Land's End and the coast path to Sennen

Walk Views

The visitor centre at Land's End is skippable; the coast path is not. Walk the 1.5 miles north to Sennen Cove along the cliff for the Longships lighthouse, the granite stacks, and the arch at Enys Dodnan, then eat at the Old Success Inn. Keep going 4 miles to Cape Cornwall for a quieter headland with no entry fee. Choughs, Cornwall's red-legged crows, returned to these cliffs in 2001.

10. Porthcurno Telegraph Museum

Museum Rainy day

From 1870 this tiny valley was the busiest cable station in the world, where 14 undersea telegraph cables came ashore and linked Britain to India, Australia and America. The museum is in the wartime tunnels dug into the hillside in 1941 to protect the station, and the original equipment still works. It explains why there is a beach cable hut and a cable marker on the sand below.

11. Looe, Polperro and the Beyond Paradise locations

Fishing town TV

Looe is a working fishing port split in two by its river, and since 2023 the setting for the BBC's Beyond Paradise, which filmed the police station on the quay. Guided location walks run from the harbour. The 5-mile coast path west to Polperro, a smaller harbour with no cars, is one of the best half-days in south Cornwall; come back by bus or by boat in summer.

Bookable: Looe Beyond Paradise TV locations guided walk (Lucy Daniel Guide · from £21.2) — live availability on FareHarbor.

Gardens, Mines and Inland

12. The Eden Project

Garden Biomes

Two giant transparent domes in a former china-clay pit outside St Austell: the Rainforest Biome is the largest indoor rainforest in the world, with a canopy walkway and a waterfall, and is kept at 35°C; the Mediterranean Biome is the cooler one with the café. The outdoor gardens cover 30 acres. Allow four hours; arrive at opening to do the Rainforest before it fills. Eden Sessions concerts run in the arena in June and July.

13. The Lost Gardens of Heligan

Garden Victorian

A 200-acre Victorian estate garden abandoned after the First World War, when most of its gardeners were killed, and rediscovered under brambles in 1990. The Jungle has a Burmese rope bridge over tree ferns and a 100-foot gunnera; the Mud Maid and Giant's Head sculptures are in the Woodland Walk. It is 10 minutes from Eden and the two are usually done on the same day.

14. Geevor Tin Mine

Mine Underground

The largest preserved tin mine in Britain, on the cliffs at Pendeen, and the last to close in the area, in 1990. The underground tour goes into Wheal Mexico, an 18th-century working, with a hard hat. The surface buildings, mill and dry (changing room) are as the miners left them. The Crowns engine houses at nearby Botallack, perched on the cliff edge, are the most photographed mine ruins in Cornwall and feature in Poldark.

15. Bodmin Moor and the Cheesewring

Moor Walk

The granite upland in the middle of Cornwall, with Brown Willy its highest point at 1,378 feet. The Cheesewring is a stack of balanced granite slabs on Stowe's Hill near Minions, a short walk from the car park past the Hurlers stone circles. Jamaica Inn, Daphne du Maurier's smugglers' pub, is beside the A30 and still serves. Dozmary Pool, said to hold Excalibur, is a tarn 5 miles south.

16. The South West Coast Path

Walk 630 miles

The longest National Trail in England runs the whole Cornish coast, and any mile of it is a day out. Three sections are the pick: Port Isaac to Padstow (Doc Martin's village, 12 miles), St Ives to Zennor (6 miles, hard, the best cliffs), and Lizard Point to Kynance Cove (3 miles, serpentine rock, the most southerly point in Britain). Buses link most trailheads in summer; the First Kernow app has them.

FAQ: Cornwall

How many days do you need in Cornwall?

Five to seven. Cornwall is long and thin and the roads are slow; a week lets you base yourself in two places, one on the north coast and one in the far west or south, without spending every day driving.

What is the best base in Cornwall?

St Ives or Penzance for west Cornwall and the Minack, Padstow or Newquay for the north coast and surfing, Falmouth for the south coast and boats, and St Austell or Fowey for Eden and Heligan. Fowey and Falmouth are the best for eating out.

Can you do Cornwall without a car?

Partly. The main line reaches Penzance with branch lines to St Ives, Newquay, Falmouth and Looe, and summer buses run along the coast. Tintagel, Minack and Land's End are hard without a car or a tour.

When is the best time to visit Cornwall?

Late May to June and September: the sea is warm enough to swim by September, the schools are in, and the roads move. July and August are full and St Ives and Padstow car parks close by mid-morning. The surf is best from October to March.
