Harry Potter Walking Tours London Without Studio Tickets

WanderEngland editorial · Published 2026-06-19

Warner Bros Studio Tour tickets sell out months in advance. If you're visiting London and couldn't snag a booking — or simply prefer to stay in central London rather than trek to Watford — there's good news: the city itself is packed with actual Harry Potter filming locations. A walking tour through these spots delivers a different but equally magical experience, and you can book one for tomorrow.

London served as a primary filming location throughout the Harry Potter series. While the studio tour shows you props and sets, walking tours take you to the real streets, markets, and landmarks where cameras rolled. You'll stand in the exact spots where Daniel Radcliffe walked as Harry, see the archway that became the Leaky Cauldron entrance, and visit the train station where Platform 9¾ lives.

The key London filming locations

Before diving into tour options, here's what you'll typically see on a Harry Potter walking tour of London — and why these locations matter to fans.

Leadenhall Market — Diagon Alley Entrance

This Victorian covered market in the City of London stood in for the entrance to Diagon Alley in The Philosopher's Stone. The optician's shop at 42 Bull's Head Passage served as the exterior of the Leaky Cauldron. The ornate ironwork and painted shopfronts make it easy to imagine wizards slipping through the mundane world into somewhere more interesting.

King's Cross Station — Platform 9¾

The famous platform where Harry catches the Hogwarts Express actually exists — sort of. There's now a permanent installation between platforms 9 and 10 featuring a luggage trolley disappearing into the wall. A Harry Potter shop sits adjacent, and staff will photograph you running at the wall with your chosen house scarf. Expect queues, especially on weekends.

Millennium Bridge — Death Eater Attack

The footbridge connecting St Paul's Cathedral to Tate Modern was spectacularly destroyed by Death Eaters in The Half-Blood Prince. It's still standing in our timeline, offering excellent views of the Thames and the cathedral dome.

Australia House — Gringotts Interior

The Australian High Commission on the Strand has an interior marble hall that served as the basis for Gringotts Wizarding Bank. You can't enter without official business (it's a working embassy), but the exterior is worth seeing and guides explain what lies inside.

Piccadilly Circus — The Escape Scene

In Deathly Hallows Part 1, Harry, Ron, and Hermione flee Death Eaters and nearly get hit by a bus at Piccadilly Circus. The bright lights and chaotic energy remain unchanged.

Borough Market — Leaky Cauldron Exterior

For Prisoner of Azkaban, the production moved the Leaky Cauldron to a different location. The archway near Borough Market's Stoney Street entrance became the new exterior. This working food market adds atmosphere and gives you an excuse to grab lunch.

Why a guided tour beats doing it yourself

You could visit these spots independently — they're all public locations and most are free to access. But guided tours offer three advantages worth the modest ticket price.

Efficiency: The locations spread across different London neighborhoods. A guide plots an efficient route so you're not backtracking across the Tube network or getting lost in the City's winding streets. Most tours cover 6–10 locations in about 2–3 hours.

Behind-the-scenes knowledge: Guides share filming details you won't find on plaques. Which scenes used CGI versus practical effects? Where did the cast eat lunch between takes? What did J.K. Rowling say when she visited the set? These stories transform random buildings into meaningful places.

Photo opportunities: Guides know the exact angles to recreate film shots. They'll position you where Harry stood and snap the picture themselves, so your whole group appears in frame. Some tours include props like wands and scarves.

Types of tours available

Shared walking tours

The most affordable option. You join a group of strangers (typically 10–20 people) led by a single guide. Tours run multiple times daily and cost roughly £17–30 per person. Duration is usually 2–2.5 hours. These work well for solo travelers, couples, and small groups who don't mind sharing the experience.

Private walking tours

Book a guide exclusively for your party. You set the pace, ask unlimited questions, and avoid waiting for other participants. Private tours cost more (often £150–250 for the whole group, not per person), but the per-person cost becomes reasonable with four or more people. Ideal for families with children who need bathroom breaks or friends who want to linger at favorite spots.

Bus tours

Cover more ground but with less walking. Bus tours typically include locations farther from central London, sometimes reaching spots in the City and South Bank that walking tours skip. Prices run higher (around £40–50 per person) but you stay comfortable and see more. Some combine bus transport with walking segments at key locations.

Self-guided with apps

Several apps offer GPS-triggered audio content as you walk. Cheaper than guided tours but you miss the live interaction, the Q&A, and the photo assistance. Better suited for repeat visitors who've done a guided tour before and want to revisit at their own pace.

What about Oxford and Cambridge?

While this guide focuses on London, keen fans should know that Christ Church College in Oxford and Cambridge University also feature in the films. Christ Church's dining hall inspired the Hogwarts Great Hall (though the actual filming used a studio recreation). Several scenes shot at New College, Oxford.

Planning tips for Potter fans

  • Book ahead in summer: July and August see the heaviest demand. Book at least a few days in advance for shared tours, a week or more for private options.
  • Wear comfortable shoes: Walking tours cover 2–3 miles on London's pavements. Trainers beat sandals.
  • Check the weather: Tours run rain or shine. London being London, bring a light waterproof layer.
  • Platform 9¾ queues: If your tour includes King's Cross, the trolley photo queue can eat 20–30 minutes. Some tours skip the queue and just show you the location. Ask when booking if this matters to you.
  • Combine with other tours: Many visitors pair a morning Harry Potter tour with an afternoon exploring other London attractions. Our London tours include history walks, food tours, and river cruises that complement the wizarding theme.

Studio Tour vs walking tours: what's the difference?

They're entirely different experiences, not substitutes.

Warner Bros Studio Tour (located in Leavesden, about 20 miles northwest of central London) shows you actual film sets, costumes, props, and special effects secrets. You'll see the Great Hall, Dumbledore's office, and Diagon Alley as built for the films. It takes 3–4 hours minimum and requires advance tickets that often sell out months ahead.

Walking tours keep you in central London and visit real-world locations used in filming. You won't see props or sets, but you'll stand where the cameras stood and walk the same streets the characters did on screen. No advance booking required — most tours have availability within days, sometimes same-day.

The ideal trip includes both, but if you can only do one, walking tours offer the more accessible option. And if you're in London without studio tickets, they're the next best thing.
