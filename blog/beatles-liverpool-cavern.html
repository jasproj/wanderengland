The Beatles, Liverpool & the Cavern Club

Where the most influential band in history found their sound — and the city that shaped them

Every band has a home. For the Beatles — the group that reshaped popular music in the 1960s and never stopped being referenced since — that home is Liverpool. John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison, and Ringo Starr all grew up in the city's working-class neighbourhoods, formed their early versions of the band here, played hundreds of shows in its venues, and carried the city's particular dry wit and maritime character into everything they made.

Liverpool's Beatles heritage isn't a fabrication. The streets, venues, and houses are real and genuinely worth visiting — especially for anyone who grew up with the music.

The Cavern Club

Mathew Street — where a manager walked in and changed everything

The Cavern Club opened in January 1957 in a warehouse cellar at 10 Mathew Street, originally as a jazz venue. The Beatles made their Cavern debut in February 1961 — by which point they had already honed their act through hundreds of shows in Hamburg's clubs. Over the next two and a half years they played the Cavern a documented 292 times. It was during a lunchtime show here that local record shop manager Brian Epstein first saw the band, becoming their manager and securing the record deal that launched Beatlemania. The current club (rebuilt in 1984 on the same site) still hosts live music nightly and is the focal point of the Mathew Street music district.

10 Mathew Street · Free entry most lunchtimes · Live music nightly

The Beatles Story

The most comprehensive Beatles exhibition anywhere

At the Albert Dock, the Beatles Story museum traces the full arc of the band's career — from skiffle sessions in Woolton to the rooftop concert at Apple Records. The recreated sets include a Hamburg club, a replica of the Cavern, and the Abbey Road studio. Original artefacts, instruments, and personal items are displayed throughout. It's substantial enough that most visitors need two to three hours. The Albert Dock itself — five Grade I listed Victorian warehouses around a dock basin — is one of the finest examples of industrial architecture in England.

~£18 adults · Allow 2–3 hours · Albert Dock
Childhood homes tip: The National Trust runs guided tours of John Lennon's home (Mendips) and Paul McCartney's home (20 Forthlin Road). Slots are limited and sell out well in advance — book as soon as you know your travel dates. They are genuine homes, not recreations, and that makes them unexpectedly moving.

Penny Lane & Strawberry Field

The streets that became songs

Penny Lane is a real street in the Mossley Hill area of south Liverpool, running past the landmarks mentioned in the song — the barber's shop, the shelter in the middle of the roundabout, the fire station. The street sign is heavily photographed and regularly stolen. Strawberry Field is around two miles north, in Woolton: the red gates of the former Salvation Army children's home appear on the cover of the Imagine exhibition. Both are accessible to visitors; a Beatles-themed bus route connects the main sites.

Both accessible via the Magical Mystery Tour bus
