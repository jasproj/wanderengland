Harry Potter Walking Tour Oxford vs London: Which Should You Choose?

If you're a Harry Potter fan planning a UK trip, you've probably wondered: should I do the Harry Potter walking tour in Oxford or London? Both cities offer incredible film locations, but they deliver very different experiences. This guide breaks down what each location offers so you can pick the right tour for your trip.

What Oxford's Harry Potter Tours Actually Include

Oxford serves as the most-authentic Harry Potter filming location in the UK. The city's university colleges were used extensively in the films, and many locations remain exactly as they appeared on screen. The Oxford tours page features several Harry Potter-specific options led by Oxford alumni who know the campus intimately.

Christ Church College is the star of the show. This is where they filmed the Great Hall, and walking through its dining hall feels like stepping directly into the movies. The hall itself isn't just a set piece—it's a functioning college dining room, which means you'll see real students eating dinner under the same wooden beams that appeared in the film.

The Bodleian Library, specifically the Duke Humfrey's Library section, served as the Hogwarts Library in several films. You cannot enter all areas during a walking tour, but the exterior shots and nearby Radcliffe Camera are recognizable immediately. Your guide will point out exactly which angles were used for different scenes.

New College appears in several scenes as well. Its medieval cloisters were used for hallway shots, and the architecture is distinctly "Hogwarts" in a way that feels more authentic than purpose-built film sets.

Oxford tours typically run 2-3 hours and cost between £28-45 per person. The walking pace is relaxed, but you'll cover about 2 miles on uneven cobblestones and through college courtyards. Wear comfortable shoes and bring a rain jacket—English weather doesn't care about your fandom level.

What London's Harry Potter Tours Offer

London's Harry Potter walking tours take you through the Muggle world that connects to the Wizarding World. While Oxford focuses on the academic Hogwarts settings, London's tours highlight locations used for the Ministry of Magic, Diagon Alley, and the Knight Bus sequences. The London tours page has dedicated Harry Potter options that cover ground the Oxford tours simply can't reach.

Leadenhall Market is the real-world Diagon Alley. The Victorian covered market, with its ornate ironwork and glass roof, was used for the magical shopping street. It's particularly striking at night when the lights come on.

Platform 9¾ at King's Cross Station is a must-visit for any Harry Potter fan. You can pose with the luggage trolley disappearing into the wall, and there's an entire shop dedicated to wizarding merchandise. This isn't part of most walking tours—it's easy to visit independently—but guides will often include it in their route.

London tours tend to be longer (3-4 hours) and cover more ground using the Tube. Prices range from £35-60 depending on group size and whether you want a private guide. The advantage is seeing multiple film locations across the city, but the downside is more time spent traveling between sites.

Key Differences: Oxford vs London

Authenticity: Oxford wins for fans who want to see actual filming locations that still exist today. London offers more variety but many locations are just "this building appeared briefly in the background."

Time commitment: Oxford is a 1-hour train ride from London Paddington, making it a perfect day trip. If you only have one day free, you're choosing between seeing multiple London locations or the most iconic Oxford spots. If you have two days, doing both is ideal—start with Oxford for the pure Hogwarts experience, then London for the broader wizarding world.

Walking level: Oxford is more walking-intensive but in a compact area. London covers more distance but includes Tube travel between stops. Neither is particularly challenging, but Oxford's cobblestones and college steps are harder on the knees.

crowds: Oxford's college-based tours are more contained and feel less rushed. London's tours can feel like a scavenger hunt across a busy city. Summer months (June-August) are packed in both cities, but Oxford's smaller scale makes crowds feel more intense in courtyards.

Which Tour Should You Book?

Choose Oxford if:

  • You want the most authentic Hogwarts locations
  • You're short on time and need a concentrated experience
  • You're interested in Oxford University history beyond Harry Potter
  • You want a more intimate, guided experience

Choose London if:

  • You want to see the broadest range of film locations
  • You're already spending multiple days in London
  • You want to combine Harry Potter sites with other London attractions
  • You're interested in how the films transformed real London landmarks

Practical Booking Tips

Both cities offer shared (group) and private tour options. Shared tours in Oxford start around £28-34 per person and are a great value. Private tours run £80-150 for a group of up to six people. The private option is worth it if you're traveling with die-hard fans who want to ask lots of questions or customize the route.

Book at least 2-3 weeks in advance during summer months. Oxford college access can be restricted during exam periods (usually mid-to-late May), so check current schedules. Most tour operators offer free cancellation up to 24-48 hours before the tour.

Consider combining your Harry Potter tour with other activities. Oxford's walking and punting combo tours let you see the city from both land and river. In London, some tours include pub stops where filming took place—perfect for adult fans who want to experience the wizard pub culture firsthand.

The Bottom Line

There's no wrong choice between Oxford and London for Harry Potter fans. Oxford delivers the iconic Hogwarts settings that feel frozen in time. London provides the broader wizarding world and more variety in filming locations. Most serious fans end up doing tours in both cities—start with Oxford for the academic magic, then London to see how the franchise transformed ordinary London spots into wizard landmarks.

If you can only do one, Oxford offers a more focused experience that justifies the day trip from London. The combination of Christ Church's Great Hall, the Bodleian Library, and walking through actual university grounds creates an experience that dedicated fans remember for years.
