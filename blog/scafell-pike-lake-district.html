Scafell Pike & Wordsworth Country

England's highest summit and the landscape that made the Romantic movement

The Lake District contains England's highest mountains, its largest lakes, and the landscape that William Wordsworth and his contemporaries turned into a defining image of the English countryside. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2017. Walking the fells here — from the gentle shores of Windermere to the rocky summit plateau of Scafell Pike — is one of the defining English outdoor experiences.

Two things above all others draw visitors to the heart of the Lake District: the climb to Scafell Pike at 978 metres above sea level, and the village of Grasmere where Wordsworth wrote his greatest poems and where the light on the water still looks exactly as his poetry describes it.

Scafell Pike

England's highest mountain — and a serious day out

Scafell Pike stands 978 metres above sea level in the western Lake District, near Wasdale Head. The summit is a broad rocky plateau with a cairn and, on clear days, views across to the Scottish hills and the Irish Sea. The most popular and most rewarding route is the Corridor Route from Wasdale Head — roughly 8 miles round trip with around 900 metres of ascent. The terrain above the valley is rocky and navigationally demanding in poor visibility. This is proper mountain country: good boots, waterproofs, a map and compass, and an early start are not optional. Most people allow 5–8 hours.

978m · National Trust · No facilities on the mountain
Safety: Scafell Pike claims more mountain rescues than any other English summit. The upper plateau has limited landmarks and is genuinely easy to get lost on in cloud. Download the route to your phone, bring a paper map, and check the weather forecast before you go.

Wordsworth's Grasmere

The village where the Romantics took root

William Wordsworth moved to Dove Cottage at Grasmere in December 1799 with his sister Dorothy. The eight years he spent there were the most productive of his literary life — he wrote major poems including The Prelude, Resolution and Independence, and the first Lyrical Ballads here. The cottage is open to visitors, furnished as it was in Wordsworth's time, with the small garden that Dorothy tended. The Wordsworth Museum next door holds manuscripts, portraits, and letters. Wordsworth is buried in St Oswald's churchyard in the village centre alongside his wife, sister, and several of his children.

~£9 adults · Allow 1.5 hours for cottage and museum

Windermere

England's largest lake — busiest end and quiet end

Windermere is England's largest natural lake, around 10.5 miles long. The Bowness-on-Windermere area at the southern end is the most visited part of the national park and can be very crowded in summer. The lake's western shore, accessed by ferry from Bowness, is quieter and offers better views of the full length of the water. Hill Top, Beatrix Potter's farmhouse near Near Sawrey, is a 10-minute walk from the Far Ferry terminal and is one of the most visited National Trust properties in England — book well in advance.

Western shore ferry from Bowness · Hill Top: NT members free, ~£12 others
