Avebury Stone Circle

Older than Stonehenge, larger than Stonehenge, and free to walk through any time

Most visitors to Wiltshire go to Stonehenge. They book timed tickets, arrive by shuttle bus, follow a path around the stones at a respectful distance, and take photographs. Stonehenge is genuinely extraordinary. But Avebury — 25 miles to the north — is something different: a Neolithic monument so large that the village of Avebury sits inside it, with houses, a pub, and a manor house all enclosed by the prehistoric bank and ditch.

Avebury's outer henge is, by area, one of the largest prehistoric monuments in the world. Its outer circle originally held over 100 standing sarsen stones arranged around a circular bank and ditch roughly 430 metres across. It dates to around 2850 BCE — about 500 years before Stonehenge was completed. And it's free.

The Stone Circle

You can touch these stones

The stones at Avebury are unworked sarsens — rough, naturally shaped boulders dragged from the Marlborough Downs. They lack the engineering precision of Stonehenge's dressed trilithons, but their sheer bulk and the scale of the enclosure are striking. Many of the original stones were destroyed during the medieval period (when the church considered them pagan) or buried and later re-erected. The ones that stand are substantial — the largest reaching several metres in height. Walking among them, with the grass bank rising on one side and the village visible through gaps in the stones, feels unlike any other prehistoric site in England.

Free entry · Open 24 hours · National Trust site

The Surrounding Prehistoric Landscape

Silbury Hill and the West Kennet Long Barrow

Avebury is surrounded by other major Neolithic monuments. Silbury Hill — visible from the stone circle — is the largest prehistoric mound in Europe, around 40 metres high, built in stages between roughly 2400 and 2300 BCE. Its purpose is unknown; it has been excavated repeatedly without finding a burial or obvious function. The West Kennet Long Barrow, a ten-minute walk to the south, is a Neolithic chambered tomb dating to around 3650 BCE. You can enter the chambers through a narrow doorway and stand inside a 5,500-year-old burial monument.

West Kennet Long Barrow: free, English Heritage · 10 min drive from Avebury
Avebury + Stonehenge day: Many guided tours combine both sites in a single day. Avebury typically occupies the morning, Stonehenge the afternoon. This is genuinely one of the best days out in southern England for anyone interested in prehistory.

The Red Lion Pub

The Red Lion in Avebury village claims to be the only pub in the world inside a stone circle. Whether or not that distinction holds up under scrutiny, it makes for a good lunch stop. A ploughman's lunch and a pint while sitting within a Neolithic henge is a distinctly English experience.
