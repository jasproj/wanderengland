20 Best Things to Do in Edinburgh

Scotland's capital — castles, crags, whisky, and one of Europe's most walkable old towns

Edinburgh is a city built on volcanoes, which tells you most of what you need to know. The Old Town stacks medieval tenements along a ridge between a castle and a palace. The New Town (built in the 1760s) lays out Georgian elegance in precise grid streets below. Arthur's Seat — an actual extinct volcano — rises from the middle of it all like someone dropped a Highland peak into the city center.

It's the kind of place where you turn a corner and get a view that stops you walking. If you're visiting London, Edinburgh is 4.5 hours by train — worth every minute.

1. Edinburgh Castle

The fortress that defines the skyline

Perched on Castle Rock — a volcanic plug — Edinburgh Castle has dominated the city for over 900 years. Inside: the Scottish Crown Jewels (the Honours of Scotland), the Stone of Destiny, St Margaret's Chapel (the oldest building in Edinburgh), and Mons Meg, a massive medieval cannon. The views from the battlements stretch across the city to the Firth of Forth and the Highlands beyond.

~£19.50 adults · Allow 2–3 hours
Tip: Book online in advance — timed entry reduces queuing. The One O'Clock Gun fires daily (except Sundays) and will make you jump even when you're expecting it.

2. Arthur's Seat

An extinct volcano in the middle of the city

The main peak of Holyrood Park rises 251 meters above sea level and offers 360-degree views of Edinburgh, the Firth of Forth, and the Pentland Hills. The hike takes 45–60 minutes from the Holyrood Palace entrance. Not technically difficult, but steep and exposed near the top — wear proper shoes and bring a layer for wind.

Free · 2–3 hours round trip

3. The Royal Mile

A mile of medieval history from castle to palace

The spine of the Old Town runs downhill from Edinburgh Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse. Along the way: St Giles' Cathedral, the Writers' Museum, the Real Mary King's Close (underground streets), countless pubs, and narrow "closes" — alleyways that tunnel off both sides into courtyards and hidden stairs. It's touristy on the surface but rewards anyone who ducks into the closes.

Free to walk · Attractions vary

4. National Museum of Scotland

Scotland's story from geology to space

Free entry, world-class collection. The Scottish history galleries trace the story from the Ice Age to modern Scotland. The Grand Gallery's iron-and-glass Victorian hall is worth visiting for architecture alone. Highlights include Dolly the Sheep, Lewis Chessmen replicas, and the rooftop terrace with castle views. You could spend three hours here easily.

Free · Allow 2–3 hours

5. Calton Hill

The best sunset viewpoint in Edinburgh

A short climb from the east end of Princes Street gives you a panoramic view of the castle, Old Town, Arthur's Seat, and the Firth of Forth. The unfinished National Monument (Edinburgh's "Disgrace") and the Nelson Monument sit on top. Get there 30 minutes before sunset and you'll see why every photographer in Edinburgh knows this spot.

Free · 15 min climb

6. Palace of Holyroodhouse

The King's official Scottish residence

At the bottom of the Royal Mile, Holyroodhouse has been the seat of Scottish royalty since the 16th century. Mary Queen of Scots' chambers are the highlight — including the room where her secretary David Rizzio was murdered. The ruined Holyrood Abbey next door is hauntingly beautiful. Check the calendar — the Palace closes during royal visits.

~£18 adults · Allow 1.5 hours

7. Scotch Whisky Experience

A crash course in Scotland's national drink

Located next to the Castle on the Royal Mile, this guided tour explains the whisky-making process, takes you through a barrel ride, and finishes with a tasting. The Gold Tour includes a tasting of whiskies from all five Scottish whisky regions. The collection room holds nearly 3,500 bottles — the world's largest collection of Scotch.

From ~£19 · Allow 1 hour

8. Grassmarket

Pubs, shops, and a dark history

This sunken square below the Castle was Edinburgh's execution site for over 400 years — now it's one of the liveliest pub and restaurant strips in the city. The Last Drop and Maggie Dickson's are named after execution history. On sunny days the outdoor tables fill up with castle views overhead. The Saturday farmers' market is one of the best in Scotland.

Free to explore

9. Dean Village

A hidden village five minutes from Princes Street

Follow the Water of Leith Walkway from the west end of the city centre and you drop into Dean Village — a cluster of old mill buildings and cottages in a wooded gorge that feels like it belongs in the countryside. Cross the bridge, walk along the water, and continue to the Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art. Most visitors miss it entirely.

Free · 30 min walk

10. Scottish National Gallery

From Botticelli to the Scottish Colourists

Free gallery on the Mound between Old and New Town. The collection covers European masters (Titian, Vermeer, Monet) alongside Scottish painters like Raeburn and the Colourists. Small enough to see in an hour, large enough to hold genuinely important paintings. The building itself, with its neoclassical pillars, frames a perfect view up to the Castle.

Free · Allow 1 hour

11. Leith

Edinburgh's port neighborhood — and its best restaurants

A 20-minute bus ride from the centre takes you to Leith, once a rough port district now home to Edinburgh's best dining scene. The Shore area along the waterfront has Michelin-starred restaurants next to traditional pubs. The Royal Yacht Britannia, moored at Ocean Terminal, is one of Scotland's most visited attractions.

Bus 22 or 36 from centre

12. Real Mary King's Close

Underground streets preserved beneath the Royal Mile

When the Royal Exchange (now the City Chambers) was built in 1753, they didn't demolish the existing streets — they built over them. Guided tours take you through these buried 17th-century closes, explaining plague, poverty, and daily life in old Edinburgh. It's atmospheric and claustrophobic in equal measure.

~£19 · Tours every 15 min

13. Princes Street Gardens

A park in a drained loch

The gardens fill the valley between Old and New Town — once the Nor Loch, now one of the most scenic city parks in Europe. The Scott Monument towers over the east gardens (climb its 287 steps for views). In summer, locals picnic on the grass with the castle looming above. The Ross Bandstand hosts concerts, and the Christmas market fills the western gardens in winter.

Free · Scott Monument ~£8

14. Edinburgh Ghost Tours

The most haunted city in Europe, allegedly

Edinburgh's underground vaults, graveyards, and dark closes are the perfect setting for ghost tours. The best are run by Mercat Tours (Blair Street Underground Vaults) and City of the Dead (Greyfriars Kirkyard — locked at night, tour groups get a key). Even skeptics enjoy them — the history is real, the settings are genuinely creepy, and the guides know their material.

From ~£15 · Evening tours

15. Royal Botanic Garden

70 acres of gardens with castle views

A 20-minute walk north of the centre (or a short bus), the Botanics are free to enter and beautifully maintained. The Chinese Hillside, the Rock Garden, and the Victorian glasshouses are the highlights. The view from the top of the garden, looking back toward the castle and the Old Town skyline, is one of Edinburgh's best — and far less crowded than Calton Hill.

Free · Glasshouses ~£7.50

16. Stockbridge

Edinburgh's village-within-a-city

Walk north from the New Town and you reach Stockbridge — a neighborhood of independent shops, delis, and cafés centered on the Sunday farmers' market. The Stockbridge Market (every Sunday, 10am–5pm) is the best market in Edinburgh: cheese, street food, crafts, plants. Browse the charity shops on St Stephen Street and the antique stores on Raeburn Place.

Market Sundays 10am–5pm

17. Greyfriars Bobby and Greyfriars Kirkyard

A loyal dog and a historic graveyard

The statue of Greyfriars Bobby — a Skye Terrier who supposedly guarded his owner's grave for 14 years — sits outside Greyfriars Kirk. The kirkyard behind is one of Edinburgh's most atmospheric spots: 16th and 17th-century tombs, Harry Potter name inspirations (Tom Riddle, McGonagall), and views up to the Castle. Wander it in daylight before the ghost tours take over at night.

Free

18. Edinburgh Festival (August)

The world's largest arts festival

The Edinburgh Festival Fringe, International Festival, Book Festival, and Art Festival all run in August, turning the city into one enormous performance venue. Street performers, stand-up comedy, theatre, dance, and music in every available space — churches, pubs, shipping containers, car parks. It's chaotic, expensive, and absolutely worth experiencing once. Book accommodation months ahead.

August · Many shows free

19. Day Trip to the Highlands

Glens, lochs, and castles within a day's reach

Several operators run day trips from Edinburgh to the Highlands — Loch Lomond, Glencoe, Stirling Castle, and the Trossachs are all reachable in a day. Rabbie's and Highland Explorer run small-group tours in 16-seat minibuses. If you have a car, the drive to Glencoe (3 hours each way) through Rannoch Moor is one of the most dramatic drives in Scotland.

Day tours from ~£50

20. Edinburgh's Pub Culture

From Victorian gin palaces to hidden whisky bars

The Bow Bar (whisky), Sandy Bell's (live folk music), the Sheep Heid Inn (oldest pub in Scotland, circa 1360), and the Café Royal Circle Bar (Victorian tile work) are all worth seeking out. Edinburgh drinks well — the craft beer scene has exploded (Barney's, Moonwake) and the cocktail bars in the New Town compete with London's best.

Pint from ~£5.50
