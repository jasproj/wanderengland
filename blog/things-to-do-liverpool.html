15 Best Things to Do in Liverpool

Liverpool is a port city that peaked in 1900, collapsed, and rebuilt itself around music, football and the waterfront. It is 2 hours 15 minutes from London Euston, and the centre is walkable from Lime Street station to the Pier Head in fifteen minutes. The Beatles are unavoidable and worth it; the rest of this list is what to do once you have done them. Tours you can book here →

The Beatles

1. A Beatles taxi tour

Guided Book ahead

The sites are spread across the southern suburbs, so the black-cab tours run by Beatles-obsessed drivers are the practical way to see them in two to three hours: Penny Lane and the barber's shop, Strawberry Field gates, the Menlove Avenue house where John lived with Aunt Mimi, 20 Forthlin Road where Paul lived and 'I Saw Her Standing There' was written, St Peter's church hall in Woolton where John met Paul on 6 July 1957, and Eleanor Rigby's grave in the churchyard.

Bookable: Come Together: semi-private Beatles taxi tour (Fab 4 Taxi Tours · from £68.9) — live availability on FareHarbor.

2. The Beatles Story

Museum Albert Dock

The permanent exhibition in the basement of the Britannia Vaults at the Royal Albert Dock, with a replica Cavern, Casbah and Abbey Road Studio 2, John's round glasses, George's first guitar, and an audio guide narrated by Julia Baird, John's half-sister. Allow two hours. It is the one to do first if you know nothing; the taxi tours assume you have.

3. The Cavern Club

Live music Mathew Street

The band played 292 times in the original cellar between 1961 and 1963; it was filled in during 1973 for a railway ventilation shaft and rebuilt in 1984 using the original bricks, a few yards along Mathew Street. Live music runs from early afternoon every day, free in the front room until the evening. The Cavern Pub opposite has the wall of bricks with every band that played the original. The Beatles statue is on the waterfront by the Pier Head.

4. The childhood homes with the National Trust

Houses Minibus only

Mendips (John's) and 20 Forthlin Road (Paul's) are owned by the National Trust and can only be entered on its minibus tour from the Jurys Inn or Speke Hall, in small groups, no photography inside. Both are furnished as they were in the late 1950s, down to the front porch at Mendips where John and Paul practised because the acoustics were better. Book weeks ahead in summer; it sells out.

5. Strawberry Field

Garden Reopened 2019

The Salvation Army children's home behind the red gates on Beaconsfield Road closed in 2005 and reopened in 2019 as a visitor centre with an exhibition about the song and the home, a café, and the garden John climbed into as a boy. The original gates are inside; the ones on the road are replicas. It is 15 minutes by bus from the centre and five minutes from Mendips.

The Waterfront and Museums

6. Royal Albert Dock

UNESCO Free to walk

The largest group of Grade I listed buildings in Britain, opened in 1846 as the first fireproof warehouses in the world (brick, stone and cast iron, no wood), closed in 1972 and restored in the 1980s. Tate Liverpool is here (closed for refurbishment until 2027; its shows are at RIBA North across the way), with the Merseyside Maritime Museum, the International Slavery Museum and the Beatles Story. The dock is the evening-walk and restaurant quarter.

7. The Three Graces and the Mersey Ferry

Boat 50 min

The Royal Liver Building (1911, with the two 18-foot Liver Birds on top), the Cunard Building and the Port of Liverpool Building line the Pier Head. The Mersey Ferry's River Explorer cruise is 50 minutes across to Seacombe and Woodside and back with commentary, and plays Gerry and the Pacemakers at the end, every time. The Royal Liver Building 360 tour goes up the clock tower for the view.

8. Museum of Liverpool and the Walker Art Gallery

Museums Free

The Museum of Liverpool, the white building at the Pier Head, covers the city's history from the Overhead Railway carriage to the Liverpool FC and Everton galleries and the 1980s; the top floor has the best waterfront view. The Walker, on William Brown Street by Lime Street, is the national gallery of the north, with Hockney, Rembrandt, a Stubbs and the Pre-Raphaelites. Both are free.

9. Liverpool and Metropolitan Cathedrals

Cathedrals Hope Street

Two cathedrals at either end of the same street. The Anglican one (1904–1978, Giles Gilbert Scott) is the largest in Britain and the fifth-largest church in the world; the tower is 331 feet and the lift and 108 steps reach a platform with the best view in the city. The Catholic Metropolitan (1967), the circular one with the lantern of coloured glass, is nicknamed Paddy's Wigwam; its crypt is by Lutyens from an abandoned earlier scheme. The Philharmonic pub between them has listed Victorian gents' toilets.

10. The Upside Down House

Photo stop 15 min

A house built upside down at the Royal Albert Dock, where you walk on the ceilings through furnished rooms with the sofa and dining table bolted above your head, and the photos come out inverted. It takes 15 minutes and children ask to go again. It is the cheapest ticket on the dock.

Football, Neighbourhoods and the Coast

11. Anfield and Goodison Park

Football Tour days

Liverpool FC's stadium tour goes through the home dressing room, down the tunnel under the This Is Anfield sign and into the Kop; the museum has the six European Cups. Match tickets are near-impossible for non-members; the tour is the way in. Everton's new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock on the waterfront opened for the 2025–26 season, with Goodison kept for the women's team. Anfield is a 15-minute bus from the centre.

12. A cycle tour of the city centre

Guided Bike

Liverpool is flat and the centre is compact, so a guided bike tour covers more ground than a walk in the same time: the waterfront, the Georgian Quarter around Hope Street, the two cathedrals, Chinatown's arch (the largest outside China), the Baltic Triangle, and the Cavern Quarter. Bikes, helmets and a guide who grew up here are included.

Bookable: Liverpool city centre highlights cycle tour (Liverpool Cycle Tours Ltd · from £29) — live availability on FareHarbor.

13. The Baltic Triangle and Bold Street

Food Nightlife

The Baltic Triangle is the old warehouse district south of the centre turned into bars, Baltic Market street food, and the Cains Brewery Village. Bold Street, running up from the centre to the bombed-out church of St Luke's, is the independent restaurant street: Mowgli, Maray, Bundobust, and the Lebanese and Turkish places. Lark Lane in Aigburth is the local version, 15 minutes out by bus.

Bookable: Liverpool with a local: walking tour (Walking Tours in the UK · from £12.72) — live availability on FareHarbor.

14. Crosby Beach and Another Place

Beach Art

Antony Gormley's 100 cast-iron men, each cast from his own body, stand along two miles of Crosby beach and 1 km out to sea, facing the horizon, and are submerged and revealed by every tide. It is 25 minutes by train from Central to Waterloo or Blundellsands, then a 10-minute walk. Go an hour before low tide. The ships into Liverpool pass directly behind them.

15. Sefton Park and the Palm House

Park Free

A 235-acre Victorian park in the south of the city, with a lake, a boating lake, the Fairy Glen, and the Palm House, a three-tier glasshouse from 1896 restored in 2001 and used for concerts and weddings. Lark Lane runs off the park's eastern edge. The Mystery, in Wavertree, is where the first public park in Britain opened in 1856.

FAQ: Liverpool

How many days do you need in Liverpool?

Two full days: one for the Beatles (taxi tour, Cavern, Beatles Story) and one for the waterfront, museums and cathedrals. Add a third for Anfield or Everton's new stadium and Crosby beach.

What is the best Beatles tour in Liverpool?

The black-cab tours with a driver-guide cover the suburban sites (Penny Lane, Strawberry Field, the childhood homes from outside, Woolton) that walking tours cannot reach, in two to three hours. Pair one with the Beatles Story museum and an afternoon at the Cavern. To go inside the childhood homes, book the National Trust minibus separately.

Is Liverpool walkable?

The centre is: Lime Street to the Pier Head is 15 minutes, the cathedrals 15 minutes the other way. The Beatles sites, Anfield, Sefton Park and Crosby need a bus, train or cab.

Is Liverpool worth visiting if you are not into the Beatles?

Yes. The waterfront is a UNESCO-scale piece of Victorian engineering, the Walker is one of the best galleries outside London, the two cathedrals are extraordinary, and the food on Bold Street and in the Baltic Triangle is as good as Manchester's.
