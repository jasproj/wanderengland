16 Best Things to Do in Manchester

Manchester was the first industrial city in the world and has the mills, canals, libraries and football clubs to prove it, two hours from London Euston. The centre is compact: Piccadilly to Deansgate is a 15-minute walk through the Northern Quarter, and the tram reaches both stadiums, Salford Quays and MediaCity in twenty. Industry, Books and Buildings

1. The Science and Industry Museum

Museum Free

On the site of Liverpool Road station, the oldest surviving passenger railway station in the world (1830), with a working replica of Stephenson's Planet locomotive, the Power Hall of mill engines (reopened 2024 after a six-year restoration), and the Textiles Gallery where the cotton machinery is demonstrated. The 1830 Warehouse is the oldest railway warehouse anywhere. Free; allow three hours.

2. John Rylands Library

Library Free

A neo-Gothic cathedral to books on Deansgate, opened in 1900 by Enriqueta Rylands in memory of her husband, the city's first multi-millionaire. The Historic Reading Room is the reason to go; the collection has a fragment of St John's Gospel from around 125 AD, the oldest known piece of the New Testament, and a Gutenberg Bible. Entry is free; the building reopened in 2024 after a reworking of the entrance and galleries.

3. A city-centre walking tour

Guided 2 hrs

Manchester's history is in its buildings and a guide makes them readable: the Town Hall (reopening in 2026 after a seven-year restoration), the Royal Exchange where cotton was traded, the Free Trade Hall where Dylan went electric and was called Judas, the Peterloo memorial, Chetham's and the Northern Quarter. Two hours from the centre, small groups, run by locals.

4. Chetham's Library

Library 1653

The oldest public library in the English-speaking world, founded in 1653 in a 1421 college building beside the cathedral. Marx and Engels worked together at the window desk in the reading room in the summer of 1845, and the alcove and the books they used are marked. Entry is by guided tour on set days; book on the website. The cathedral next door has the finest medieval wood carving in the north.

5. Manchester Cathedral and the Medieval Quarter

Church Free

A 15th-century collegiate church with the widest nave in England and misericords from 1500 showing a rabbit roasting a hunter. The area around it is what survived the 1996 IRA bomb and the 1940 Blitz: Chetham's, the Corn Exchange (now restaurants), Sinclair's Oyster Bar and the Old Wellington, two timber pubs that were moved 300 metres on rollers in 1999 to make the square.

6. The Whitworth and Manchester Art Gallery

Galleries Free

The Whitworth, in a park on Oxford Road by the university, reopened in 2015 with a glass wing into the trees and has the best Turner watercolours outside London, a textile collection, and a wallpaper collection. Manchester Art Gallery, on Mosley Street in the centre, has the Pre-Raphaelites, including Hunt's The Hireling Shepherd and Waterhouse's Hylas and the Nymphs. Both free.

Music, Football and Culture

7. A Manchester music tour

Guided Taxi or on foot

The Haçienda site (now flats with a plaque), the Free Trade Hall, Salford Lads Club from the Smiths' sleeve, the Oasis sites in Burnage, Factory Records' office, Sifters record shop, and the Stone Roses' Spike Island story. The taxi version reaches Burnage and Salford in two and a half hours; the walking version stays in the centre. Both are run by people who were there.

Bookable: Manchester music taxi tour (Brit Music Tours · from £275.6) — live availability on FareHarbor.

8. Old Trafford and the Etihad

Football Tour days

Manchester United's stadium tour at Old Trafford goes through the dressing room, the tunnel and the dugout, with the museum's three European Cups; the club is planning a new 100,000-seat stadium next door. Manchester City's Etihad tour includes the first-team dressing room and the pitch-side walk. Both are 15 to 20 minutes by tram; match tickets need membership, the tours do not. The National Football Museum in the centre is free.

9. Aviva Studios and Factory International

Arts Opened 2023

The £240 million arts building on the Irwell behind the Science and Industry Museum, home of the Manchester International Festival (biennial, odd years, in July). The Warehouse is a 5,000-capacity flexible space and the Hall a 1,600-seat theatre; the programme runs year-round with large-scale theatre, dance, gigs and installations that would not fit anywhere else. The Yayoi Kusama inflatables filled it in its opening year.

10. A silent disco walk through the city

Fun 1 hr

Wireless headphones, a guide with a microphone, and an hour of dancing through the streets from the Northern Quarter to St Ann's Square while passers-by look on. It is the thing to do with a group or a hen party, and it is cheaper than a night out. Runs on weekend afternoons and evenings from the centre.

Bookable: Silent disco adventure through Manchester (Silent Adventures · from £19.61) — live availability on FareHarbor.

11. The Northern Quarter and Afflecks

Neighbourhood Shopping

The old wholesale district north of Piccadilly Gardens, with Victorian warehouses turned into bars, record shops (Piccadilly Records, Vinyl Exchange), vintage shops, and street art that changes monthly around Stevenson Square. Afflecks is a four-floor indoor market of independent stalls that has been there since 1982. Mackie Mayor, in a restored 1858 meat market on Swan Street, is the food hall.

12. A chocolate workshop

Hands-on Rainy day

Two hours of tempering, moulding and truffle-rolling in a studio in the centre, with the results boxed to take home. It works as a date, a team activity or a rainy afternoon with teenagers, and the instructors are patient with people who have never done it. Book a weekend slot ahead.

Outside the Centre

13. Castlefield and the canals

Walk Free

Where the Bridgewater Canal, the first true canal in Britain (1761), ends in a basin under a tangle of Victorian railway viaducts. The Castlefield Viaduct, a 330-metre disused one, opened in 2022 as a sky garden with planting along the deck. The Roman fort of Mamucium, from which the city's name comes, is partly reconstructed beside it. The Rochdale Canal towpath runs from here through the Gay Village to Piccadilly.

14. Salford Quays, MediaCity and the Lowry

Tram 20 min

The old Manchester Docks, closed in 1982 and rebuilt around the BBC and ITV studios at MediaCity, the Imperial War Museum North (Libeskind's aluminium shard, free), and the Lowry, a theatre complex with the largest collection of L.S. Lowry's matchstick-men paintings. The footbridge over the Ship Canal links the two sides. Trafford Centre is one stop further if you need a mall.

15. The Peak District in an hour

Day trip Train

The Hope Valley line from Piccadilly reaches Edale in 45 minutes, the start of the Pennine Way and the walk up Kinder Scout, where the 1932 Mass Trespass won the right to roam. Castleton, one stop on, has the Blue John and Speedwell caverns and Peveril Castle. Buxton, an hour by train from Piccadilly, is the spa town with the Crescent and the Opera House.

16. Heaton Park

Park 600 acres

The largest municipal park in Europe, 20 minutes north by tram, with a neoclassical hall by James Wyatt, a tramway museum running original Manchester trams at weekends, a boating lake, an 18-hole golf course, and the site of Oasis's 2009 and 2025 homecoming gigs. The Papal Monument marks where John Paul II said mass to 200,000 in 1982.

FAQ: Manchester

How many days do you need in Manchester?

Two. One for the centre (Science and Industry Museum, John Rylands, Northern Quarter, a walking or music tour) and one for a stadium tour plus Salford Quays, or a day trip to the Peak District.

Is Manchester worth visiting?

Yes, for the industrial history (the first railway station, Chetham's, the Bridgewater Canal), the music, the football, the free galleries, and the food in the Northern Quarter and Ancoats. It is cheaper than London and two hours away by train.

Can you tour Old Trafford and the Etihad on the same day?

Yes. They are on opposite sides of the city, 15 to 20 minutes from the centre by tram each, and tours run through the day on non-match days. Book both in advance; weekend slots fill.

Which area should I stay in Manchester?

The Northern Quarter or Ancoats for bars and restaurants, Deansgate and Castlefield for a quieter central base near the museums, Piccadilly for the station. Salford Quays is cheaper and 20 minutes out by tram.
