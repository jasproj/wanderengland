Private London Walking Tours: Prices, Best Routes & How to Book (2026)

A private London walking tour is a guide for your group only, two to three hours on foot, with the route set by what you want to see. Private is the right call for three kinds of visitor: families with children who will not last a 25-person group, people with a specific interest (Roman London, street art, Dickens, the Blitz), and anyone with limited mobility who needs the pace set for them. Prices on this page come from the operators' FareHarbor listings, checked October 2026, and every button opens the operator's own calendar. Travelling with kids? There is a separate guide to private London walks for families.

What a private London walking tour costs

Private walks in London are priced per group, not per person, which is why they look expensive next to a £18 Jack the Ripper walk and cheap next to a car tour. How to book a private walk

  • Book 2–3 weeks ahead for weekends and school holidays. Guides are individuals, not a pool; the good ones fill up.
  • Say who is coming. Ages, mobility, interests. A guide with 48 hours' notice will rework the route.
  • Start times: 10:00 avoids the worst of the Tube and gets you to Westminster before the coach parties; 14:00 suits jet-lagged arrivals.
  • Meeting points are usually a Tube station exit. Confirm which exit; Bank has twelve.
  • Tipping is not expected on a private walk in London but £10–£20 for a guide who went over time is normal.

FAQ: Private London Walking Tours

How much does a private walking tour of London cost?

Most private walks are £185 to £285 for the group, for two to three hours, regardless of whether you are two people or six. Semi-private walks capped at around eight people are about £55 a head. Public walks run £18 to £46 a person.

How many people can join a private walking tour?

Operators list private walks for parties of up to about eight; most will take larger groups for an extra charge per person. Tell them the number when you book so they can adjust the route for crowd-friendly stops.

Is a private tour worth it for two people?

Usually not on price alone: a semi-private walk at £55 each is cheaper than £220 for a private guide. Private wins if you want a specific theme, a particular start time, or a slower pace.

What is the best private walking tour for first-time visitors?

Westminster and royal London. It covers Parliament, the Abbey, Whitehall, Horse Guards and Buckingham Palace in one loop and can be timed for the Changing of the Guard at 11:00.

Are private walking tours suitable for children?

Yes, and that is the main reason people book them. The Harry Potter locations walk and the Roman London walk are the two most booked for families. The Jack the Ripper walk is not suitable for under-12s.
