Oxford's Dreaming Spires

The Bodleian Library, ancient colleges, and the city Matthew Arnold never quite stopped thinking about

Matthew Arnold coined the phrase in 1865 — "that sweet city with her dreaming spires" — and it has been attached to Oxford ever since. Look at the skyline from the top of Carfax Tower and you understand immediately why it stuck: a medieval cityscape of stone towers, domes, and pointed gables rising above college walls, apparently unchanged for centuries.

Oxford has been a university town since the late 12th century. The 38 colleges that make up the university are scattered across the city centre, each with its own architecture, gardens, hall, and chapel. For visitors, navigating them is part of the pleasure.

The Bodleian Library

One of Europe's oldest and most beautiful libraries

The Bodleian is Oxford's main research library and one of the great library buildings in the world. Founded in its current form in 1602, it is a legal deposit library — receiving a copy of every book published in the UK. The Divinity School (1427) is its oldest teaching room and was used as the Hogwarts hospital wing and infirmary in the Harry Potter films. Duke Humfrey's Library above it, with its painted ceiling and chained medieval manuscripts, served as the restricted section at Hogwarts. Guided tours are the only way to access these interiors — they book out, especially in summer.

The Radcliffe Camera

Oxford's most distinctive building

The Radcliffe Camera (1748) is a circular domed reading room now used as a library reading room — it is not normally open to the public, but it dominates Radcliffe Square and is photographed from every angle by every visitor. John Radcliffe, a physician who made his fortune as royal doctor, left money to fund it. The name comes from the Latin camera for a vaulted room. Standing in Radcliffe Square with the Camera on one side, the Bodleian on another, and the spire of St Mary's rising above — this is the Oxford of imagination, and the reality matches it.

Exterior free · Square open at all times
Best view: Climb the tower of the University Church of St Mary the Virgin for one of the most photographed views in England — the Radcliffe Camera directly below, ringed by college buildings and spires. It costs a few pounds and is worth every step.

Christ Church

The college that inspired Hogwarts — and where Lewis Carroll taught

Christ Church is the largest and most visited of Oxford's colleges. Its Great Hall — the college dining room — directly inspired the design of Hogwarts Great Hall in the Harry Potter films, though the actual dining scenes were shot at Leavesden Studios. Tom Quad is the largest quadrangle in Oxford; Tom Tower above the gate was completed by Christopher Wren. The college's Cathedral is the smallest cathedral in England and doubles as the college chapel. Lewis Carroll (Charles Dodgson) was a mathematics lecturer here; Alice Liddell, the inspiration for Alice in Wonderland, was the daughter of the college dean.

~£18 adults · Book timed entry · Allow 1.5 hours

Harry Potter Locations

Oxford's connection to the Harry Potter films is deep. Beyond the Bodleian and Christ Church, New College's cloisters appeared as Hogwarts corridors, and the Divinity School's stone-vaulted ceiling features in several scenes. A guided Harry Potter walking tour of Oxford visits all the key filming locations with context that a self-guided walk cannot provide.
