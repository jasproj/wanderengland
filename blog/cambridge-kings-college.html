King's College Chapel & the Cambridge Backs

Fan vaulting, Christmas carols, and the view that defines the English university

There is a view in Cambridge that appears on more postcards, screensavers, and biscuit tin lids than almost any other in England: looking across the River Cam towards the Backs, with the stone bridges in the middle ground and King's College Chapel rising behind them. It is one of those views that the reality actually exceeds.

Cambridge has been a university town since around 1209, when scholars migrating from Oxford established themselves here. The 31 colleges that make up the university are woven through the city centre, and unlike most English cities, the historic core is almost entirely given over to academic buildings, chapels, libraries, and gardens.

King's College Chapel

The finest fan vault in England

King's College Chapel was founded by Henry VI in 1441 and took around a century to complete, spanning the reigns of several monarchs. Its defining architectural feature is the ceiling — the largest example of fan vaulting in the world, a geometric canopy of stone ribs spreading from the pillars to meet in the middle of the nave. The scale is extraordinary: the chapel is 88 metres long and the vaulting spans it in one uninterrupted stone ceiling. The original medieval stained glass windows are intact on both sides. The chapel is open to visitors most days; the annual Christmas Eve Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, broadcast by the BBC, takes place here.

~£13 adults · Closed during services and exams

The Cambridge Backs

The view that defines the city

The Backs are the gardens and grounds of six colleges — King's, Clare, Trinity Hall, Trinity, St John's, and Queens' — that back directly onto the west bank of the River Cam. Walking along the Backs from Garret Hostel Bridge to Silver Street in spring, with the college buildings reflected in the water and the Chapel tower visible above the trees, is one of the genuinely lovely walks in England. It is free, takes about 30–40 minutes, and works equally well in grey November and sunny May.

Free to walk · Best with a punt for the river view
Punting tip: The view of King's College Chapel from a punt on the Cam is better than the view from the bank. A guided tour punt includes a chauffeured punter and commentary on the colleges — useful for first visits. Self-hire punts are cheaper if you're happy to navigate yourself (and capsize occasionally).

Trinity College and St John's

Two more unmissable colleges, side by side

Trinity College is the largest college at Cambridge, founded by Henry VIII in 1546. Its Great Court is the largest enclosed courtyard in the world — the scene of the Great Court Run, a race to circumnavigate the court while the clock strikes noon (famously depicted in Chariots of Fire, though the film used a different location). The Wren Library at Trinity, designed by Christopher Wren and completed in 1695, contains original manuscripts by Milton, Newton, and Tennyson. Next door, St John's College has the Bridge of Sighs — a covered stone bridge over the Cam modelled loosely on its Venetian namesake.

Trinity ~£3 adults · St John's ~£12 adults
