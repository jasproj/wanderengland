Warwick Castle

A thousand years on the River Avon — the fortress that made and unmade English kings

Warwick Castle has occupied its clifftop above the River Avon for nearly a thousand years. The fortification began as a motte-and-bailey ordered by William the Conqueror in 1068; the towers you see today were built primarily in the 14th century, during the period when the Earls of Warwick were among the most powerful men in England. The castle's towers, battlements, and Great Hall make it one of the most complete medieval fortresses in the country.

It is also the castle most associated with one of the most extraordinary careers in English medieval history: Richard Neville, the 16th Earl of Warwick, known to history as "Warwick the Kingmaker."

The Medieval Fortress

Towers, battlements, and the Great Hall

The main towers — Caesar's Tower and Guy's Tower — date from the 14th century and are among the finest examples of medieval military architecture in England. Caesar's Tower, with its unusual trefoil plan and three storeys, stands roughly 45 metres tall. The views from the battlements cover the River Avon, the town of Warwick, and the Warwickshire countryside. The Great Hall was rebuilt in the 17th century in the Gothic style and contains weapons, armour, and portraits. The State Rooms preserve a late 17th-century apartment associated with the visit of King James II in 1687.

Warwick the Kingmaker

The most powerful nobleman in medieval England

Richard Neville, the 16th Earl of Warwick, dominated English politics in the 1460s. Through a combination of vast inherited wealth, military skill, and political cunning, he became the most powerful magnate in England. He was instrumental in placing Edward IV on the throne in 1461 after defeating the Lancastrian forces. When he fell out with Edward, he switched sides, restored the Lancastrian Henry VI in 1470, and forced Edward into temporary exile. Edward returned, and Warwick was killed at the Battle of Barnet in 1471. The Kingmaker exhibition at the castle dramatises this period of history in period rooms with wax figures — somewhat theatrical, but reasonably accurate.

Kingmaker exhibition included in admission
Trebuchet demonstration: The castle operates a full-scale working trebuchet — a medieval siege engine that can fling heavy projectiles considerable distances. Demonstrations are scheduled on certain days; check the castle website. It is genuinely impressive in operation.

The Grounds and River

Capability Brown gardens and the mill island

The grounds were landscaped by Lancelot "Capability" Brown in the 1750s — he created the gentle contoured park leading down to the river that surrounds the castle today. A peacock garden, a rose garden, and the wooded slopes between the towers make for pleasant walking. The mill island in the River Avon, just below the castle walls, is a good spot to see the castle's exterior reflected in the water.
