Tintagel & the King Arthur Legend

Clifftop ruins, Atlantic wind, and 900 years of the world's most enduring legend

The north Cornwall coast near Tintagel is the kind of place where legend feels entirely plausible. Sheer black cliffs drop into a churning grey Atlantic. The ruins of a castle cling to a headland that is almost an island, connected to the mainland by a narrow footbridge with a long drop on either side. On a stormy day, standing among those walls, it is easy to understand why medieval storytellers placed the greatest of all English legends here.

Tintagel's connection to King Arthur dates to the 12th century, when Geoffrey of Monmouth wrote in his History of the Kings of Britain that Arthur was conceived at Tintagel through the magic of Merlin. The legend has been embellished by every generation since. The site itself, however, tells a more complicated and in some ways even more interesting story.

The Archaeological Reality

A 5th-century stronghold that predates the legend

Archaeological evidence shows that between the 5th and 7th centuries CE — the period when a historical Arthur, if he existed, is thought to have lived — Tintagel was an important stronghold and trading centre, importing wine, oil, and fine pottery from the Mediterranean. A stone found during excavations bears the name "Artognou" (a Celtic name sometimes linked speculatively to Arthur), though archaeologists are cautious about drawing conclusions from it. The headland was already abandoned and ruinous when Richard of Cornwall, brother of Henry III, built his castle here in the 1230s — drawn specifically by the Arthurian association.

Managed by English Heritage · ~£16 adults

The Castle Ruins

Half on the mainland, half on an island

The standing remains are primarily 13th-century — Richard of Cornwall's castle, built to lend prestige to his lordship of Cornwall by association with legend. The ruins are divided between the mainland and the headland, connected now by a modern footbridge. The climb up to the headland involves steep steps and is physically demanding. The views from the top — down into the Atlantic cove, along the cliff face in both directions — are among the finest on the English coast. Merlin's Cave, accessible at low tide, runs through the base of the headland.

Steep climb · Wear sturdy footwear · Allow 2 hours
Timing: Arrive early — Tintagel is one of the most visited English Heritage sites and the car park in the village fills quickly in summer. The walk from the village to the castle takes around 15 minutes along a lane.

Glastonbury and the Wider Legend

Tintagel is Arthur's birth-place in the legend. Glastonbury is his burial place — or rather the Isle of Avalon where he was taken after his final battle, according to medieval sources. The two sites together form the beginning and end of a legend that has never really gone away. For anyone interested in the mythology of early Britain, visiting both is worthwhile.
