14 Best Things to Do in Oxford

Oxford is 38 colleges and a city wrapped around them, an hour from London by train, and small enough to walk end to end in twenty minutes. The colleges are the point, but they keep their own hours, charge their own entry and close for exams in May and June, which is why a guided walk led by a graduate is the most efficient first morning. The Colleges and the University

1. Christ Church

College Film location

The largest college, founded by Cardinal Wolsey in 1525, with a cathedral inside it and the Great Hall that the Hogwarts dining hall was modelled on (the film used a set; the staircase below it is the real one in the films). Tom Tower rings Great Tom 101 times at 9.05pm every night, one for each original scholar. Lewis Carroll taught maths here; the Alice window is in the hall. Book a timed ticket online; walk-ups queue.

2. A university walking tour with a graduate

Guided 2 hrs

The practical way in. Guides who studied here can get you into quads that are shut to independent visitors and explain the tutorial system, formal hall, sub fusc and why the colleges are richer than the university. Two hours covers the Radcliffe Square cluster, Christ Church from outside, and usually New College's cloisters. Shared tours run daily from the centre; private ones can be routed around Harry Potter or Inspector Morse locations.

3. The Bodleian Library and Radcliffe Camera

Library Book ahead

The Bodleian has held a copy of every book published in Britain since 1610 and now holds 13 million. The Divinity School (1488), with its fan-vaulted ceiling, was the Hogwarts infirmary; Duke Humfrey's Library above it was the restricted section. Both are on the guided tours, which sell out days ahead. The Radcliffe Camera, the round reading room from 1749, is only visitable on the extended tour. The Weston Library opposite has free exhibitions.

4. New College and its cloisters

College Gardens

New in 1379. The cloisters and the 700-year-old holm oak in them appear in the Goblet of Fire; the chapel has an Epstein Lazarus and the stained glass Reynolds designed. The gardens are enclosed by the only surviving stretch of the medieval city wall, which the college is obliged to let the Lord Mayor inspect every three years. The entrance on Holywell Street is easy to miss.

Bookable: Harry Potter film locations walking tour including New College (Alumni Tours · from £34.99) — live availability on FareHarbor.

5. Magdalen College and Addison's Walk

College Deer park

The college with the 144-foot bell tower at the bottom of the High Street, where the choir sings at 6am on May Morning to a crowd of thousands on the bridge below. Magdalen owns a deer park, and Addison's Walk is a mile-long path around a water meadow where C.S. Lewis, a fellow here for 29 years, had the conversation with Tolkien that led him back to Christianity. Snake's-head fritillaries flower in the meadow in April.

On and Off the Water

6. Punting on the Cherwell

River Summer

You stand at the back and push a 20-foot flat-bottomed boat with a pole; it is harder than it looks and the Cherwell is shallow enough that falling in is only embarrassing. Hire from Magdalen Bridge Boathouse or the Cherwell Boathouse and head upstream, away from the bridge crowd, to the Victoria Arms pub at Marston. Chauffeured punts with a guide take the work out of it and can be combined with a walking tour.

7. The Covered Market

Food Since 1774

Oxford's market has traded under one roof since 1774 and still has butchers hanging game in season, a cobbler, and the Cake Shop where the window shows wedding cakes being iced. Ben's Cookies started here in 1984. Brown's Café is the cheap breakfast; Pieminister and the Alpha Bar are the lunch queues. Entrances are on the High Street and Market Street.

8. The Ashmolean Museum

Museum Free

Britain's oldest public museum, opened in 1683, with the Alfred Jewel, Powhatan's Mantle, Guy Fawkes's lantern, and the Messiah Stradivarius, which has barely been played since 1716. The Pre-Raphaelite room and the Egyptian galleries are the crowded ones. The rooftop restaurant is the best view over the college roofs in the city and does not require a ticket.

9. Pitt Rivers Museum

Museum Free

Reached through the Natural History Museum (dodo remains, dinosaur skeletons, a Victorian iron-and-glass roof), the Pitt Rivers is a single dim hall of 500,000 objects arranged by type rather than culture, in the original 1884 cases with handwritten labels. The shrunken heads were taken off display in 2020; the rest, from Inuit kayaks to witch bottles, is intact. Open from noon.

10. The Oxford University Press and Jericho

Neighbourhood Canal

North of the centre, Jericho is the Victorian printers' and canal-workers' district turned into the city's bar and restaurant quarter. Walton Street has the Phoenix Picturehouse (1913, the oldest cinema in Oxford), the OUP's neoclassical building, and St Barnabas, an Italianate church on the canal that Hardy put in Jude the Obscure. Walk back along the Oxford Canal towpath to Hythe Bridge.

Pubs, Views and Day Trips

11. The Eagle and Child, the Turf and the Bear

Pubs History

The Eagle and Child on St Giles is where the Inklings (Tolkien, Lewis) met on Tuesday mornings from 1933; it has been closed for refurbishment and is due to reopen under new owners. The Turf Tavern, hidden down an alley off Holywell Street, is where Bill Clinton did not inhale. The Bear, from 1242, has 4,500 club ties nailed to the walls, each traded for a half-pint.

12. Carfax Tower and the view

Viewpoint 99 steps

The 14th-century tower of the demolished St Martin's church at the central crossroads, with 99 steps up to a platform over the High Street. No building in central Oxford may be taller than it. The University Church of St Mary the Virgin on Radcliffe Square has the better view, 127 steps up its 13th-century tower, directly over the Camera and All Souls. Both charge a few pounds.

13. Blenheim Palace

Day trip 20 min

The S3 bus from the city centre reaches Woodstock and Blenheim in 25 minutes. Vanbrugh's baroque palace was the nation's gift to the Duke of Marlborough in 1705 and is where Churchill was born; the park by Capability Brown is 2,000 acres and the Column of Victory walk is the one to do. A ticket converts to an annual pass if you ask. Combine it with the Cotswolds on a day tour from London or Oxford.

Bookable: Cotswolds, Blenheim Palace and Oxford private day tour (London Country Tours · from £630.7) — live availability on FareHarbor.

14. Port Meadow and the Trout at Wolvercote

Walk Free

A 300-acre common on the Thames that has never been ploughed, grazed by horses and cattle under rights that predate the Domesday Book. Walk north from Jericho along the river for an hour to Wolvercote and the Trout Inn, which Inspector Morse drank at, then back on the other bank via the ruins of Godstow Abbey, where Fair Rosamund, Henry II's mistress, was buried in 1176.

FAQ: Oxford

Can you visit Oxford colleges without a tour?

Many open to visitors in the afternoon for a fee, but hours change weekly, some close for exams from mid-May to late June, and the famous ones (Christ Church, Magdalen, New College) run timed tickets. A guided tour with an alumni guide gets into quads that are otherwise shut and saves the research.

Is Oxford a good day trip from London?

Yes. Trains from Paddington take just under an hour, and Marylebone trains take about 70 minutes; both stations are a 10-minute walk from the centre. A full day covers two colleges, the Bodleian, the covered market and a punt.

Oxford or Cambridge?

Oxford is bigger, has more to do beyond the colleges (Ashmolean, Pitt Rivers, Blenheim, better pubs) and is easier from London. Cambridge is smaller, prettier along the river, and better for punting. If you want the Harry Potter locations, it is Oxford.

Where do you punt in Oxford?

Magdalen Bridge Boathouse at the bottom of the High Street, or the Cherwell Boathouse in north Oxford. Hire by the hour, or book a chauffeured punt with a guide if you would rather not pole it yourself.
